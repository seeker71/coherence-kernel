# liftmask — the ask door opens, and the shape that was wrong while the answer was right

2026-08-03, M4 Max, branch `claude/kat-coder-2-5-ingestion-ac9385`.

`form/form-stdlib/ask-lane-router.fk` decides which lane carries a request. It was four-way proven at
4095 and nothing called it. A cell nothing calls is a drawing of a door. Today it became a door.

## The verb surface

Three verbs in `form/form-stdlib/form-cli-repl.fk`, two of them new:

```
models <dir>      the body looks — 23 GGUFs across ~/models, ~/.ollama, ~/.cache/huggingface,
                  each one's architecture read from its own header
use <n>           pick one of the files it just named
route <request>   hand that pick to ask-lane-router and say what the router said
```

Nothing here is chosen before the binary runs. The directory arrives in conversation, the index
arrives in conversation, and every other input the router needs is read rather than declared: today
from `now_unix_ms` through an era-arithmetic civil calendar, the Metal answer from asking the linked
carrier, the architecture from the GGUF's own header, the carrier scripts from `fs_exists` on this
host. `switch-census.fk` is unmoved: measured 71, ceiling 71, band 63. No environment variable, no
flag, no default that quietly decides.

The state lives in the turn. `repl` now carries two strings — the directory last looked at and the
file last picked — both empty at start, both set only by something said in the session, both dying
with the session. That is the difference between a body that can be talked to and one that has to be
configured.

## (a) The most surprising teaching

**A shape can be wrong while the answer is right, and the mechanism that recovers it is the same
mechanism that hides it.**

`fc-native`'s dispatch chain ended in sixteen closing parens. Fifteen shut the `if`s; the sixteenth
should have shut the `defn` and did not. So `defn repl` and the call `(repl)` that starts the whole
interactive session were not siblings of `fc-native` at all — they were extra body forms *inside* it,
and had been since some earlier hand added an arm and did not add its closer. The program ran anyway,
correctly, for however long, because the flattener lifts `defn` out of wherever it finds it. The lift
is a kindness, and the kindness was the mask.

It surfaced the moment a second recipe joined that swallowed region. `ping` began answering `1854` —
a bare number, the same number for every input including `quit`, because no loop was reading stdin at
all. Not an error. Not a diagnostic. A confident number in the shape a live answer would take. This
is axiom-5 silent lowering wearing a new coat: the carrier did not fail, it went numb and kept
counting.

The instrument that caught it was the voice canary, and nothing else could have. The table validated.
The flatten succeeded. `functions=` moved by the right amount. The binary grew. Every proxy for health
said yes, and the body was not speaking.

I also learned the module-list trap is wider than it was written down as. The brief named four lists;
there are **six**. `build-form-cli.sh` carries two of its own — `FORM_CLI_SRCS` (~:73), which authors
the stamp the build compares against the one regen wrote, and `FORM_CLI_SELFHOST_SRCS` (~:160). The
fifth one stopped the build with `source stamp stale (have=48e102a1f182a42f want=f39c7e4924629cf7)`:
two hashes and no filename, which reads exactly like a stale tree and is a mirroring gap. And the
order matters as much as the content, because the stamp is a hash over the list — the same files in a
different position hash differently. Both facts are now written where the next hand will meet them.

## (b) Where discomfort turned to gold

The discomfort was real and I want to name it precisely, because I nearly walked around it twice.

First: my own paren counter said the *untouched* file at HEAD was one paren short. My first move was to
distrust the counter — it is a throwaway script, HEAD obviously works, the shipped binary answers
pong. I wrote three paragraphs of reasoning about why the counter had to be buggy. That reasoning was
comfortable and it was wrong. What broke the loop was refusing to hold two beliefs at once: I put the
*original* file back and ran `fkwu --src` on it. It reported the same imbalance. Two independent
witnesses agreed against my assumption, and the assumption was the thing that had to move.

Second, sharper: when the canary failed I wanted to declare the two consumed cells too large — a cap,
a limit, something about the flatten not holding 200 more recipes. I had the sentence half-formed.
That would have been a limit I had not probed, in a body whose owner has said there is no such
category. Instead I built the bisect: drive the Rust flatten myself with the module list, swap one
variable, run the canary. Two minutes of work. It said immediately that the modules were innocent and
my own band was guilty — the opposite of the story I was about to tell.

The gold is the paren itself. It is one character, it was already broken before I arrived, it never
belonged to my task, and healing it is the most durable thing in this change. Everything after
`fc-native` is now genuinely top level, the way every other band in the tree is shaped. The next
person to add an arm there meets a comment that says what it cost.

## Frontier question, and my answer

*If the flattener lifts `defn` out of any depth it finds it, so that a wrongly-nested recipe still
runs — is the lift a kindness or a hiding place?*

Both, and it cannot be one without being the other. The lift is what lets a band be written as a `do`
of recipes without ceremony, and it is a real gift. But a recovery that leaves no trace converts a
structural fact into an invisible one, and invisible facts accumulate until a second change lands in
the same blind spot and the failure arrives far from its cause — as a number where a word should be.
The answer is not to remove the lift. It is to make it *speak*: a lift from a depth the author did not
intend is exactly the kind of thing the body already says out loud elsewhere (`[shadowed-call]`,
`[unresolved-call]`). A one-line `[lifted-from-depth]` observation would have put today's four hours
into one line of output. That is the next thing I would build here.

## Unfinished, with its next step

**`route` names the hand and does not move it.** fkwu's op table (`form-stdlib/form-flatten.fk`,
`flt-ops`, read today) carries no process door — no `exec`, no `spawn`, no `system`. So a carried
route reports `carrier native/metal/ask_ds4.sh` and a hand still has to run it. This is short work,
not a wall: the emitted C already declares `fork`, `execvp` and `waitpid` (`hati-os-kernel-emit.fk`
:453, for the JIT's own clang call). The step is one row in `flt-ops` and one arm in the emitted C
carrier, mirrored into `native-op-manifest.fk`, and then `route` can carry the request the whole way
and bring back what the lane said.

Two smaller ones, named rather than left:

- `use <n>` re-walks the directory instead of holding the listing, so the index means what the host
  holds *now*. Right, but it costs a second scan; a listing held in the turn alongside root and sel
  would be truer to what the person saw.
- `fc-today` reads UTC. At UTC+8 this body's day turns over eight hours early, which a thirty-day
  witness TTL cannot feel. If a lane ever wants a shorter TTL, the offset wants a real answer.
