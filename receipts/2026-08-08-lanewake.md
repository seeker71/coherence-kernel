## Where the time was, and what moved

The dense lane's own receipt said it plainly: attestant kernels only, one thread per matvec
row, serial right-fold, serial encoder, no tuning. qk-matvec-lane.fk had already measured WHY
that is slow — the serial matvec is ALU-bound on per-weight scale recomputation, not
bandwidth-bound, moving 2 GB at ~25 GB/s of a ~477 GB/s machine — and the llama lane had
already proven the two remedies on 2026-08-04 without ever sweeping them into the family cell.
This stone swept them, one commit each, stream-gated at every step.

**Change A — the bit-exact twins, family-wide** (`dense-token-handle.fk`): tpg=256 on every
mode-0 dispatch, concurrent batches with per-dispatch `barrier_before` declared by the cell,
`form_rmsnorm_tg_f32` inside its n<=4096 radius (the 32B/72B norms at d=5120/8192 stay on the
serial attestant — past the radius the twin is wrong, not slow), `form_rope_pair_f32` (hd
even), two-stage argmax (comparison and selection only, ties still lowest-index). Equality,
not epsilon.

**Change B — every matvec takes the lane fold** (`dense-token-handle.fk`): one SIMD group of
32 per row, lane l folding columns l, l+32, ... with k counting DOWN so the within-lane fold
keeps the right-fold direction; per-block scale invariants hoisted (identical f32 values,
association untouched); `metal::simd_sum` the one reassociation, bounded by qk-matvec-lane's
named epsilon at parts=32. Q6_K/Q4_K lanes are that cell's, Q8_0's is q8-0-msl.fk's, and
Q5_K/Q5_0/Q4_0 lane kernels are new in the dense cell — each transcribed from its attestant's
own weight decomposition, split at the block invariant and nowhere else.

stream for TWO prompts per model ("The capital of France is" / "Once upon a time"), captured
before the change and demanded equal after. `form/native/metal/dense-stream-witness.fk` (new)
prints both streams for any dense blob.

## The op-count analysis (Urs's second sentence)

Per token, llama-3B needs ~3.21 GMAC of matvec work (28 layers x 100.7 MMAC + a 394 MMAC
head) and that count did not change — what changed is the ops that were being performed
BESIDE it:

* The serial Q4_K matvec decoded the superblock's two f16 scales once per WEIGHT — each a
  ~15-iteration multiply loop — so ~30 redundant ALU ops rode on every useful
  multiply-accumulate. The hoist computes them once per 256 weights: identical values,
  identical association, billions of ops per token simply gone. This is the purest form of
  "reduce the ops needed": the answer cannot change because the removed ops produced values
  the kept ops reproduce exactly.
* The serial one-thread argmax scanned 128 256 logits at device-memory latency; the two-stage
  split performs the SAME comparisons with the same tie rule.
* Nothing position-invariant is recomputed or re-uploaded: weights are mmap'd once, rope
  freqs resident, per-token host traffic is 4 bytes out.
* NOT taken, named for the next stone (qk-matvec-lane's header carries the measurements):
  factoring d*sc out of the inner sum, accumulating in quantized space, bitmask decode,
  float4 vectorization — each changes the association materially and belongs to a rung that
  states its own bound. That rung is the remaining distance to the floor.

## Most surprising teaching

The serial matvec was never bandwidth-bound. The floor table put llama-3B 273x off the floor
and the reflex is "memory access", but qk-matvec-lane.fk had already measured the truth this
stone only had to READ: 2 GB moving at ~25 GB/s of a 477 GB/s machine while the ALU recomputes
a per-superblock constant once per weight. The 18.6x came from believing the body's own recorded
measurement over the standing folklore — the most expensive experiment of this session had
already been run and written down eleven days ago, and the family cell simply had never taken
what the llama lane proved. The speedup was sitting in the tree, attested, waiting to be
dispatched.

## Discomfort to gold

The baseline sweep raced my own edits in this shared checkout, and for a few minutes the 72B
"baseline" was circular — captured by the very code it was meant to gate. The discomfort was
real: the whole stream gate rests on the before-side being untouched. The gold: metal_status's
`pipelines=` count turned out to be a pipestamp — 23/27/33 named exactly which body produced
each capture, pre-change / bit-exact twins / lane fold — and the 72B was re-baselined from a
HEAD worktree. The contaminated capture then became the AFTER witness, and its streams matched
the true baseline on both prompts: the race, once witnessed instead of stepped around, handed
over a stronger proof than the plan had.

## Frontier

**Question:** when two versions of one body run in the same checkout minutes apart, what tells
their outputs apart after the fact — and can that be relied on rather than lucked into?

**Answer, witnessed today:** the run's own status carried enough of the recipe to name the
recipe. `pipelines=23/27/33` was never designed as provenance, but a counter that moves with
the code IS a version mark, and it arbitrated three generations of captures beyond dispute.
The reliable form of the luck: a capture that will serve as a witness carries a stamp of the
body that produced it, or it cannot serve — and the door's counters already stamp for free.
Read them at capture time, not after the doubt arrives.
