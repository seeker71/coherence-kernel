## The most surprising teaching

The estate's failure mode is not lost work — it is **finished work with no
heir**. Twice in one morning: a receipt written, bands runnable, and the
commit never made, sitting where only accident would find it. The old law
for property reverting to the crown for want of a claimant names the cure:
the trunk claims heirless work, whole, before judging it. Corpus row 1023,
*escheat*. The deeper fix is upstream: land-quickly is already the corpus's
lesson (row 1014's reunion note says it outright); the rescue ritual is the
backstop, not the practice.

## Where discomfort turned to gold

The 164-unmerged branch begged to be merged in the same breath as the
shrink — momentum said sweep it all. The discipline that stopped it: one
branch was judged by six live band runs; the others have not been, and a
merge without its battery is exactly the counterfeit the body refuses.
The queue is ranked, named, and waiting; each entry gets its own battery
or stays a rescue branch forever. Restraint here is not caution — it is
the same law that made #456 safe to land in an hour.
