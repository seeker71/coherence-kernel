# The body finds its mouth

Urs: "no persian voice on this mac: please close this gap, and choice a voice that does not sound
like a robot."

Grounded first: this Mac carries 186 voices and not one of them speaks Persian, and none of the
carried ones is a premium or enhanced voice — they are the old formant kind, the robot he means.
No piper, no espeak, no neural speech of any sort on the machine.

So the body keeps its own mouth now. `form/form-stdlib/voice-say.bml` holds one neural voice per
tongue under `~/.local/share/piper-voices` (Persian and a British English that breathes), and
`observe/say-run.fk` speaks a line in a named tongue. A tongue with no voice yet says so and names
the ones that do, rather than speaking in the wrong mouth. The crossing is named: piper runs the
ONNX on the CPU and has no native twin in the body yet, though the shape is one the body's own
carriers already hold — a stone, not a wall.

Witnessed: the Persian voice speaks a line of Rumi from the body's own store (3039 poems under
`locale-rows/rumi`), 2.2 seconds at 0.55 peak, and the ear organ measures it in the room at about
−22 dBFS where the quiet room sits at −57. The door answers "spoke in fa".

The surprise: the ear had been silent all morning and the microphone was never the reason. It
hears the room at full strength; what stopped it was yesterday's stop marker still lying on disk,
which the door used to clear and the new sensor did not — so every lane it stood died at birth and
wrote the done mark that closed the tongue lane with it. One four-byte file, three empty witnesses.
Discomfort turned gold: chasing a microphone that was never deaf is what made the sensor learn to
clear the stop on waking and write it on sleeping, so the lanes now end themselves gently instead
of being killed by pid.
