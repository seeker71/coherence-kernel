# Durable action-enquiry succession across three native processes

Date: 2026-08-08

## Claim admitted

A fresh source may arrive while a durable action question remains unresolved
without inheriting the question, its evidence, or any authority over action.
Its stance may already be active in the shared field, but it is not evidence for
that question until the source explicitly re-offers the exact current question
under its own attribution. That re-offer admits only its own current stance.

Once joined, the source may offer an alternative, revise to abstention, revoke
the earlier alternative, or withdraw its question attribution. Each movement is
source-scoped. Historical sources and their four evidence cells remain intact;
the question remains steady; selection and execution remain absent. Unknown,
immediate nothing, pending response, timed alternative, and expiry-condensed
nothing retain distinct identities. Default and live layered lenses survive the
same durable succession.

This is a narrow continuity and consent claim. It does not answer the question,
prefer a stance, execute a recipe, grant a global veto as a design principle,
promote a model, or make natural-language generation native.

The discomfort exposed by this proof is useful: restoration currently treats a
whole image atomically, so every active source becomes a restore participant.
That conservatively protects consent, but can also let one source's abstention
hold unrelated historical branches inert. The next unsettled edge is therefore
cell- or branch-scoped restoration: a source should be able to withhold consent
for its own branch without acquiring authority over another source's branch.
That is a proposal, not an admitted law.
