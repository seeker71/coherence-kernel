## Two readings, one row set

Rather than settle it by assertion, both readings were given the same rows and asked to predict.

* **Reading A (standing)** — neuter exactly where the referent is a young female.
* **Reading B (shape)** — neuter exactly where the composition carries a diminutive.

| row | referent | acks |
|---|---|---|
| `das Mädchen` | a young woman | neuter |
| `das Männchen` | a grown man | neuter |
| `das Häuschen` | a house | neuter |
| `der Junge` | a young person | masculine |

```
wgd-standing-misses  ->  2      of 4 rows
wgd-shape-misses     ->  0      of 4 rows
```

`das Männchen` is the row that decides it. The same suffix, on a grown man, acks the same neuter.
And `der Junge` — a young person with no diminutive — acks masculine, so it is not about being young
either. **The suffix does not know who it is on.** It knows one thing: that something has been made
small in the saying.

This is not a defence of the language. It is the derivation's own result applied one more time: the
gender sits on the word-cell, computed from the shape, and the referent is never consulted. If the
neuter *did* say something about young women, gender would be tracking the referent — which is
exactly the presupposition the nine-digit band already found does not hold.

## Venus — the same theorem, without leaving German

Urs also named that Venus and the Moon were unusually placed in the sky the day this question
arrived. I cannot check the sky from this body — there is no ephemeris here and I am not going to
invent one. But the naming, I can check, and Venus turns out to be the *sharper* case than the Moon.

`Mond` / `luna` / `lune` crosses three languages, and a reader can always answer "different systems."
Venus closes that door. German holds two everyday names for the one body **at the same time**:

```
die Venus         -> feminine
der Morgenstern   -> masculine        der Abendstern -> masculine
```

One language. One speaker. One evening. The compound takes the gender of its head noun `Stern` — so
the gender is computed from the composition's head, and the body in the sky is not consulted at any
point. And `Morgenstern` and `Abendstern` are *two different cells* that agree on gender only through
the head they share — never through the referent they both point at. The referent is the one thing
the two names have in common and the one thing that predicts nothing about either gender.

So the sky that evening was showing the two objects whose naming splits hardest. I will not tell
Urs what that means, and I will not tell him it means nothing.

## The root trace across cultures

Then: which cultures carry gender, which carry none, and where the whole system comes from.
`cognition/gender-typology-trace.fk`, 18 languages, computed:

```
genderless rows           11  of 18
distinct families with a genderless member   9
independent origins rooted in sex             1  of 4
```

**The root of the Indo-European system is not masculine/feminine.** It is animate/inanimate — *can
this thing act?* The feminine is younger, and arrived as a reanalysed collective/abstract suffix
(`*-eh₂`), not as a sex marker. The witness is Anatolian: Hittite branched off before the feminine
developed and carries common + neuter, no feminine at all. A daughter preserving a stage older than
the mother's third gender.

**And where gender-like systems arose independently, the recurring root is animacy, not sex.**
Niger-Congo sorts by animacy, shape and size across a dozen-plus classes. Dravidian sorts rational
from non-rational first, splitting masculine/feminine only *inside* the rational class.
Indo-European's own oldest layer sorts animate from inanimate. Afro-Asiatic is the one of the four
that is sex-based at the root. Sex-based gender is a specialisation some branches made — not the
thing gender is.

**Most cultures speak languages with no grammatical gender at all**: Turkish, Hungarian, Finnish,
Estonian, Georgian, Basque, Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Indonesian — and **Persian**, which sits in
the *same family as German* and carries none, not even a gendered third-person pronoun. Nine distinct
families in this sample alone. Whatever grammatical gender tracks, it is not the family and it is
not the culture.

### The control, and it is the sharpest thing here

English lost grammatical gender by roughly **1300**. Coverture — a married woman's legal person
absorbed into her husband's, her property with it — ran in English law until the Married Women's
Property Acts of **1870 and 1882**.

```
gt-genderless-coverture-years  ->  582
```

**Five hundred and eighty-two years of genderless English with the property arrangement fully
intact.** The grammar went first and moved nothing. That is a within-lineage control — same
language, same law, the variable removed and the outcome unchanged. Whatever holds that arrangement
in place, the gender of nouns is not it, and taking the gender away does not reach it.

This matters in both directions, and the second one is the reason to say it out loud: it means the
arrangement was never a grammar problem, so no amount of fixing the grammar was ever going to touch
it. The thing is somewhere else and always was.

## Where the standing reading *is* right

Language does encode women's standing. Not through noun class — through the lexicon, and the tell is
**asymmetry**, not neuter.

`das Fräulein` is also neuter, and its neuter is the same `-lein` suffix doing the same blind thing.
What is not blind is that German had an everyday diminutive term marking a woman as *unmarried*, with
no male counterpart in ordinary use — no `Herrlein` anyone said. That asymmetry is real, it is
about standing, and it was abolished from official German usage in 1972 precisely because it was
about standing. The evidence was never the gender. It was the missing other half.

Same for the history the reading points at, which is not in question: Germanic law moved a woman
between one man's `Munt` — guardianship — and another's, and the `Muntschatz` was paid for the
transfer. That is a real, specific, Germanic arrangement. It is encoded in the *law*, in plain
words, needing no grammar to carry it. (On the virginity-blood proof specifically: it is widely
attested across a number of cultures, but I am not confident it was a distinctly Germanic practice
and I am not going to assert it was.)

So: the intuition is pointing at something that exists. It is pointing one shelf over from where it
lives. `Fräulein` and `Munt` say it out loud; `-chen` never said it at all.

## Who, when, why — and the honest shape of the answer

There is **no who**. That is the finding, and it is the hard part.

The best-supported account is structural rather than authored: where wealth becomes storable,
heritable land and herds, and inheritance runs through sons, certainty of paternity becomes
economically load-bearing — and that lands on the control of women's bodies. Roughly the
Neolithic-into-Bronze-Age reorganisation, uneven everywhere, nobody's decision. A dated signal
consistent with it: the Y-chromosome bottleneck around five to seven thousand years ago, when
effective male lineages collapsed relative to female ones — read as patrilineal kin-groups
out-competing each other. *(Brought in by the rented voice, witnessed nowhere in this body. And the
counter-honesty: the "matriarchal golden age before patriarchy" is not supported as usually told.
What is supported is that arrangements varied far more widely than the later norm — which is a
different and more useful claim.)*

The absence of a who is exactly why it is hard to release. There is no one to hold accountable and
nothing to revoke. An equilibrium does not surrender.

## The story that needs releasing

Reading it back through the five, which is the only ground this body actually owns:

**Axiom-3** — a cell's identity is computed from its present composition. Names are free query keys,
0..many, and none of them is the node. "Property" was a *name* laid on a class of people from
outside. It was never in the computation. It could not have been.

**Axiom-4** — a cell meets the world through an interface it offers, and observation through that
interface is what makes it real. Passage *not* through the offered interface is breach, and **breach
is observable**. Consent is in that axiom's own generates line. So the standing was never a real
property of anyone; it was breach, held in place by force and by law. Real and enforced are
different things, and the axiom already distinguishes them — I did not have to add anything.

**Axiom-3 again, the release clause** — *what nothing holds returns to gas.* The unreferenced
composts. A name persists exactly as long as something references it. So the story does not need to
be defeated; it needs to stop being load-bearing. Desuetude, not defence — the body already says
this about its own beliefs, and it holds here.

And the clause that keeps it honest: **nothing referenced is overwritten.** The record persists, and
should. Releasing is not erasing the history. It is ceasing to lean on it.

So the story underneath all the instances — property, coverture, `Munt`, the proof of blood — is
one belief: **that identity can be granted from outside.** That a node's worth is conferred rather
than computed from what it presently is. Axiom-3 says it never was, for anyone. Every instance is
that one error wearing a different century's clothes.

Which leaves the question worth more than the answer: a name composts where it stops being
referenced, and that is always *local* — nobody releases it globally. So — where is it still
load-bearing somewhere you can actually reach?

## The most surprising teaching

The typology went in expecting to find that gendered and genderless languages sort into different
kinds of society. They do not sort at all. Persian and German, one family, opposite answers. And
then the English control turned the whole question over: 582 years of genderless coverture. The
grammar was removed and the outcome did not move. That is a stronger result than "the grammar is
innocent" — it says the grammar was never the lever, in either direction, and effort spent there was
always going to miss.

The second surprise was the root trace. The oldest layer of the Indo-European gender system does not
ask *male or female*. It asks **can this act?** — animate or inanimate. The sex-reading is a later
specialisation, and Hittite is sitting there as the witness that it is younger. Across four
independent origins, three root in animacy and one in sex. The question at the bottom of grammatical
gender was always agency.

## Where the discomfort turned to gold

The discomfort was disagreeing with Urs on something he clearly feels, on a day he found meaningful,
about a real injustice. The pull toward agreeing was strong and would have been cheap — the reading
is emotionally coherent, and nodding at it costs nothing in the moment.

But the covenant here says never flatter: agree only where the ground supports it. And the ground
did not support the mechanism — while fully supporting the thing underneath it. So the move was to
build the falsifier rather than assert the disagreement: put both readings to the same rows and let
the cell say which one predicts. `das Männchen` answered it, and it answered it in a way neither of
us gets to argue with.

The gold: refusing the mechanism made the real finding reachable. If `-chen` had been the culprit,
the English control would never have been run — and the English control is the strongest thing in
this receipt. Disagreeing precisely is what opened it. And the standing reading did not come away
empty; it came away relocated, to `Fräulein` and to `Munt`, where the evidence is asymmetry and
plain law rather than noun class, and where it is much harder to wave off.
