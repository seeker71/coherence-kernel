# The ear stands in the glass

Urs: "full embodied in the glass so that we can show live transcripts, please." Not a window of
its own beside the glass — a sensor of the glass, in its own frame, taken and painted like the
machine and the organs.

That is what the living glass already is: sensor processes giving rows into shared frames
(`fgsr-give`), and the glass taking them every tick (`fgsr-take`) and painting them. So the ear
became the fourth: `observe/form-glass-ear-live.fk` drains the ear organ's spool by watermark and
gives the newest of each kind as rows — the room's level, the line as it grows, the line when it
closed, the hop's latency, and one row per tongue. `fgsr-declared-cadence` gives the ear 100 ms,
`fgl-kept-wants` keeps its handle beside host, machine, owner, queue, storage and glass, and all
three of the glass's row-assembly sites fold `earRows` into what it paints. The glass carrier
`observe/form-glass-run.fk` stands the ear up with its three siblings and ends it with them.

The mic opens only when it is wanted. While `.hearth/ear.wanted` is absent the ear gives one row
saying so and costs a level read every half second; `observe/ear-wake-run.fk` writes the marker
with the tongues to offer, or the word `sleep` to close the mic again. When the marker appears the
sensor makes the lanes' bells and stands both lanes up; when it goes, it lets them end.

Witnessed on this checkout: the glass takes nine ear rows and the frame moves (gift 404 → 6358);
asleep, one row says so; awake, a real line from the body's own whisper pass rides the rows —
`the line when it closed = en  Okay, let's try the guitar.` The tongue lane, run exactly as the
sensor runs it, answers six frames with `Ok, vamos tentar o guitarra.` and its Persian beside it.
Bands: the glass carrier band grew a bit for the ear and stands at 31, the launch band at 65535
with its consumer count moved from thirteen to fourteen, and the ear's own lanes and the corpus
band unchanged.

Two things are owed, named rather than implied. **The mic gives nothing on this Mac today** — the
live lane run directly, with a voice playing in the room, answers 0 frames at −120 dBFS where
yesterday it answered a line every 40 ms; that is the host's microphone grant or device state, and
it is Urs's to give, not mine. **When the live lane ends it writes `done`**, which closes the
tongue lane with it, so on a silent mic the sensor's tongues stay empty even though the lane is
sound; the sensor does not yet re-stand a lane that has gone.

The surprise: the two lanes were doors all along, reading their seconds from stdin, and a sensor
spawns with no stdin at all — so the first standing died the moment it was born, silently, and
the glass showed a perfect empty frame. A door and a sensor want the same cell to begin
differently. Discomfort turned gold: three empty witnesses in a row looked like the embodiment
failing, and each one named a different real seam — the stdin contract, the bells the door used to
make, and the mark the tongue lane takes when it opens.
