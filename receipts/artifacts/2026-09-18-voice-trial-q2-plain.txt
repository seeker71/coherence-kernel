That sounds really hard, and the dread you're feeling makes complete sense. When you can already hear the exact words someone will say, it's easy to feel defeated before the conversation even starts. Here are some thoughts that might help.

**Give yourself permission first.** You're allowed to make choices about your own holidays. Not coming home for a holiday is not abandonment. Abandonment is leaving someone without care or support; you're making one decision about one occasion, and you're clearly still thinking about her feelings. Getting clear on that in your own mind before you talk will help you stay steady if she pushes.

**Tell her sooner rather than later.** Waiting makes the dread grow and can make her feel like the news was hidden. Giving her time also gives her space to adjust and make other plans.

**Choose how you deliver it.** A phone or video call is usually better than text for something emotional, but if you know she'll escalate in real time, a thoughtful message followed by a call can also be reasonable. Do what lets you say it clearly and kindly.

**Keep it simple and warm.** You don't need a long justification; over-explaining can invite debate. Something like:

"Mom, I want to let you know now so it's not a surprise: I'm not going to be able to come home for the holidays this year. I know that's disappointing, and I'm sorry. It's not about you or about not wanting to see you. I'd really love to find another time to be together, and I'll call you on the day."

**Expect the "you're abandoning me" line, and plan your response.** You don't have to argue her out of her feelings, and you don't have to accept the label either. You can acknowledge without agreeing:

"I can hear that you're hurt, and I understand this is hard. I'm not abandoning you. I love you, and I'm choosing not to travel this time. Both of those are true."

If it escalates, it's okay to say, "I don't want this to turn into a fight. I'm going to let you have some time with this, and I'll call you in a few days." Then follow through.

**Offer something concrete, if you can.** A visit at a different time, a video call during the holiday, sending a care package, or a specific plan can help her feel she's not simply being cut out.

**Don't take responsibility for managing her reaction.** You can be kind, honest, and loving. You can't control whether she's upset. Her disappointment is real, but it doesn't mean you did something wrong.
