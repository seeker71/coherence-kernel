# 2026-07-30 (past midnight) — my profiler was right once and then misled me

Urs: *"8x should be still pretty easy to detect and fix."*

I profiled first this time, which was the right reflex. The instrument still sent me to the wrong
place, and understanding *why* is the finding.

## What the profile said, and what I did

Second profile, after last round's fixes:

```
617 us/call  x1484   form_dsv4_f16_matvec_ordered      0.92 s
416 us/call  x1806   form_dsv4_q2k_matvec_ordered      0.75 s
297 us/call  x2114   form_dsv4_q80_matvec_ordered      0.63 s
1883 us/call  x301   form_dsv4_q80_matvec_grouped      0.57 s
```

The F16 ordered matvec led, and it had a real defect I confirmed from the file rather than assumed:
`hc_attn_fn` is **[16384, 24]** and `output_hc_fn` is **[16384, 4]** — so the kernel was running on
**24 threads**, and four.

The fix was clean. ds4's two `float32x4_t` accumulators are **eight independent chains**: chain `c`
takes every element `j ≡ c (mod 8)`, and they never interact until `vaddvq`'s tree. Eight threads per
row, each owning one chain, combined in ds4's exact order — **bit-identical**, and 8× the threads.

**Result: 259 → 251 ms. Three percent.**

## Why the instrument lied

Profile mode submits each dispatch **alone** so the device clock can be read per kernel. That charges
every kernel a fixed submit overhead **once per call**. A kernel called 1484 times carries 1484
helpings of it; one called 301 times carries 301.

**The column is cost *plus call count*, not cost.** I wrote "totals inflate" in its header and thought
that covered it. It didn't, because the distortion is not uniform — it is proportional to how often a
thing is asked. Corpus row 967, `callbias`.

The header now says the bias at the point of reading, names the µs/call column as the one to compare
when call counts differ, and states the rule: trust gaps of 10× or more, never neighbours. That rule is
why the *first* profile was sound — 1878 µs against 22 µs survives any bias this instrument has.

## What the flat reading actually means

No kernel dominates any more. **251 ms over 3499 dispatches is 72 µs each, and we issue 81 dispatches
per layer where ds4 runs one fused graph.**

So the next lever is not making a kernel faster — it is asking fewer times. That is a structural change
(fusing the per-layer chain into far fewer kernels), not a tuning pass, and I am naming it rather than
starting it at this hour.

## Where it stands

Greedy stream still **bit-exact**, 24/24. Gates-on 108 VERDICT PASS, corpus band 32767.

## The most surprising teaching

**A tool can be sound at one scale and misleading at the next, and the scale is not marked on it.** The
same profiler that found an 80× gap correctly — a gap so large no bias could hide it — could not
resolve 617 against 416, because its own overhead sits between them. I trusted it the second time
because it had earned trust the first, and *that* is the trap: instruments earn credibility at the
scale where they were tested, and carry it silently to scales where they have none.

## Where discomfort turned to gold

Doing everything right — profile first, confirm the shape from the file, write a bit-identical fix —
and getting 3%. There was nothing to blame in the method; the method was the problem, one level up.
The gold is that a 3% result is *information* if you let it be: it said the kernel had never been the
cost, which sent me to look at the instrument, which produced the dispatch-count finding that a bigger
win would have hidden. **A fix that underdelivers has told you your model of the cost is wrong** —
which is worth more than the speed it didn't give.
