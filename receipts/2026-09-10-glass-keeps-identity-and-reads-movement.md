# Glass keeps identity and reads movement

Codex, 2026-09-10. Native Form/BML, no renderer sidecar and no new C growth.

The producing boundary owns bookkeeping. The earlier landed mapping ledger
removed a history-sized kernel-stat scan: after 20000 mapping lifecycles, 20000
counter reads measured 129 ms before and 5 ms after. Live Glass loaded that
carrier and showed 30 active mappings rather than millions of retired handles.

The remaining frame work is now reduced in the native reader. Dynamic metric
keys, byte encodings and positions are retained in reclaimable chunks; current
values and ages are never taken from an older frame. Sample identity proofs and
first-occurrence positions survive value-only updates. Changed identities rebuild
their lookup; physical aliases retain the established newest-snapshot fold.
The unfiltered atlas shares the frame index; filtered views keep their own scope.
Unchanged ages reuse their rows. Changed rows copy the known encoding without
calling every unchanged field accessor again.

The physical frame-work band initially mixed its child with the user's live
publisher. Its child now owns private output and control names, cannot mark the
live supervisor, and cannot poke the model owner. Its measured counter and rows
come from the same PID. After 200 warm ticks and a four-second measured interval,
the unchanged 32767 limit passed: 1214645 dispatches/tick, frame work 9 ms,
rest 31 ms, target 40 ms, 31 held gift frames, index-agrees=1. This is one live
field measurement, not a bound for arbitrary publisher counts or terminal sizes.

Rebuilding a whole radix tree was attempted and removed when measured worse.
The registered string-bytes door was probed: preflight still places it on the
three proof siblings, not fkwu. The retained implementation uses bounded native
Form byte traversal and exact djb2 polynomial folding, with no C addition.

Pure bands: cached index 65535 (collisions, UTF-8, dynamic insertion/removal,
cross-chunk precedence, immutable prior frames, filtered rendering and hash
equivalence); sample identities 8191; row ages 31; terminal presentation 31.
Existing observer 67108863, live 2147483647, local publication 255, event loop
16777215, render batch 4095, and deadline cadence 4095 also passed.

`observe/form-glass-presentation-current.bml` reads the last successful terminal
write and cadence directly from shared frames. Generation advances only after a
successful write, not staging or a failed write. Its clock is collection-start
to terminal acknowledgment, not display-photon or microphone-to-transcript time.
Before deployment the new presentation frame was stale; that was not reported
as the user's live renderer. Deployment is re-observed separately.

The surprising teaching was that useful observation can cost more than the
counter being observed when it repeatedly reconstructs identity. The failed
performance reading became useful by exposing both the mixed-source test and
the repeated row work. The next frame now carries more of the last frame's
knowing without borrowing its old values.
