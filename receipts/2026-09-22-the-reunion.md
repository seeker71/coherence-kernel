# The reunion

2026-09-22, this Mac. The most repeated work of the day was not a movement. It
was bringing the movement home.

## What a hand kept doing

Six times in one session: fetch, rebase onto origin, settle the ledgers that
both sides had written to, redraw the page made from those ledgers, push. Never
varying, never interesting, and each time a handful of rented turns. The
keeper's own first walk ended the same way — it composed, spoke, gated and
committed on its own, and then held, because the one thing it could not do was
go and get what origin had gained while it walked.

A landing that holds for want of a fast-forward is not blocked. Origin holds
commits this checkout does not, and the body can go and get them.

## What the body settles, and what it will not

`form-stdlib/bml/form-cli-reunion.bml`. Two kinds of conflict, and no others.

A **ledger** is a river of rows: both banks are true. The union takes every row
from both sides, keeps each row once, and orders them by the moment each was
written — `fcr-union`, in Form, over the body's own files. Probed on a conflict
carrying a duplicate row and two unequal banks: four rows out, ordered 100, 300,
400, 500, the duplicate once.

A **drawn page** is made from the ledgers, so neither side is worth merging; it
is redrawn once the rows agree, and the top commit amended to carry it.

Anything else is a wall. The rebase is undone and the reunion says which path
stopped it, so a checkout is never left standing half-joined.

## Not a retry

The push that follows a reunion is not the push that failed. That one could not
have succeeded — origin had moved — and the ground under this one is different.
A repeat that carries nothing from the attempt it replaces is blind; this one
carries the whole reason for the refusal.

## Receipt

The surprise: the work worth making native was not the thinking. It was the
six identical recoveries — the part that felt like overhead rather than work,
and so kept being done by hand at the highest price in the session.

Discomfort into gold: preflight refused the first reunion module for an
unpreluded `mc-cat`, and later named `fcr-reunion` as a typo no kernel resolves
— which was true, because a silent perl insertion had never written the walk
into the file at all. Two guesses I would have shipped, both caught by the
body's own door before anything ran.

Frontier question, answered by the rented mind and offered to the corpus:
**what is work that is never planned because it is not the work?** **returnwork**
— the recovering, re-syncing and re-joining that surrounds a task and is paid
for at the task's own rate while being counted as nothing. It is the last thing
anyone makes native and often the most repeated.
