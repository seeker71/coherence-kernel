# kitgate — models as rows, parity as a witnessed kit

2026-09-01 midday. The directive: more than one model at a time, each on
par with any other, no special casing — which model serves what, and what
treatment it gets, fully data-driven.

## What stands

`form-stdlib/model-registry.bml` (band 511): every standable model is a
row — name, artifact path, roles (answer, review, fast-answer, code,
input/lower). No row states an architecture: arch is read from the gguf
itself (heldname law). Which token-handle lane serves an arch, and
whether that lane is wired into the session door today, is one table
with a wiredness bit — kat's qwen35moe and llama now refuse typed,
naming their missing door, instead of dying on a literal qwen35 gate.
Readiness (seal, tokfast index, crystal) is read off the disk beside
the artifact, per piece, before any admission.

Consumers wired the same hours, so the registry is never a nameplate:
the session door's arch gate reads the lane table; the compare door and
BOTH preparation doors (tokfast freeze, crystal freeze) resolve registry
names in place of their hardcoded Q8 paths; a hearth birth handed an
empty model line resolves its model by role, path stays an override.
The byte-identical duplicate fcms-open-context was removed.

## The comparison, like for like

One door, two twins of one mind. qwen38-q8, kit complete, entered by
the live cursor and admitted in 97 seconds. qwen38-q4, crystal absent,
fell to the scanner fallback both times: 18-24 contended minutes and
forty exclamation marks — not a broken model, a missing kit piece; the
fallback's ids are not a prompt. The session door's own refusal word
named the gate: crystal-absent. The tokfast freeze alone did not open
the live path — the crystal gates it. The Q4's tokfast was frozen
through the generalized door (sealed/published/current); its crystal
freeze is running through the same one-door-for-all shape as this
receipt lands.

Named honestly, not glossed: the compare door's generation lane needs
chat framing before its words mean anything (the Q8 answered a bare
prompt with "<FAIL>" and an early stop); pace divided by budget
overclaims on early stops (divide by actual generated ids); the
fallback admissions' minutes are dominated by an unisolated cost —
whole-artifact seal verification is the suspect.

## Most surprising teaching

"No special casing" did not end in code at all. The special cases were
living in three places nobody had named: a duplicate function, two
preparation doors welded to one artifact's path, and — deepest — the
KIT beside each artifact. Two models are equal exactly when their
sidecars are, and the door's honest fallback makes a missing sidecar
look like a broken mind. Forty exclamation marks were the sound of a
missing file.

## Where discomfort became gold

The first garbage run wanted a quick blame: bad quant, wrong kernels.
Sitting with the readiness readout instead of the reflex — reading
WHICH pieces stood beside each artifact — turned "the Q4 is broken"
into "the Q4 is unprepared", and the fix from a kernel hunt into two
freeze doors that now serve every model by name.
