# The body does translate

2026-09-10. Urs, on a sentence I had committed the day before: *"The body can't translate and doesn't
pretend to: it can, we have multiple levels and we can translate 100% local for sure, and we can bring
home into form native as much as it is healthy which is probably a high percentage."*

He was right, and the sentence was mine, and I had written it without probing — which is the one thing
this body's practice says never to do.

## What was already there

`nl-translate.fk` and `nl-tongues.fk`: fourteen tongues as GRAMMAR DATA over one engine, normalizing
onto one language-neutral pivot that speaks the body's OWN symbols — n0, n1, … — so no tongue is
privileged. `nl-many-band` proved it FOUR-WAY at 67108863 across Latin, Cyrillic, CJK, kana, Arabic and
Devanagari. `locale-registry.fk` holds 64 locale seats from Urs's 2026-07-17 directive that English
stop being dominant, each with an honest proof stamp: 12 four-way, 9 fkwu, 6 three-kernel, 37 pending.

I had built a tuning channel on the insight that a phrase is a cell and a language is a face at its
address — and the body had that exact shape one level down, at the word, proven four-way, for months.
**I built the upper storey and called the ground floor impossible.**

## The gradient the body already models

The correction is not "it can" in place of "it cannot". Both are too coarse, and the shelf already
carries the honest form: every surface has a PHASE. `gas` is a surface the rented mind seeded, `water`
is one in motion as the corpus and native speakers work it, `ice` is settled. So the question was never
can-it-or-can't-it but HOW SETTLED is this face — which is exactly "as much as is healthy", and it is a
reading rather than a promise.

The tuning's faces now carry that vocabulary instead of my invented source/rendered labels: English is
`ice/transmitted`, Persian is `gas/rented-oracle-2026-09-10`, and the band refuses a face claiming a
phase it has not earned.

## Persian came home

The fa seat in the locale registry said "tongue-shelf row awaited", `pending`. It is seated now, and
the cost tells you the shelf was built right:

```
(defn nl-fa () (nlm-g-end-cop "است"))
```

One line. The Arabic script class already parsed — `(p-run "letter")` takes any byte ≥ 128 — and
`nlm-g-end-cop` already generated Persian's word order for Hindi and Turkish. A tongue is a copula and
a lexicon, not a new path. The lexicon is seven words, phase `gas`, and one choice worth overturning:
یاخته over سلول for *cell*, the native compound rather than the French loan.

```
fa parse     == the one pivot?  1
fa roundtrip == the one pivot?  1
fa says the pivot back:         بدن یاخته است
```

`nl-many-band` 67108863 → **268435455**, fifteen tongues. The locale registry's pending count 37 → 36,
and its band caught that immediately, which is what a count pin is for.

## Not owed after all: the four-way

I wrote into the band header that the four-way re-witness was "owed at the new number and claimed
nowhere in this file as already done" — which felt like the scrupulous thing and was the wrong thing
to leave, because owing a check is only honest when you cannot run it. `go`, `cargo` and `node` were
all on this host. Built all three arms and ran them:

```
fkwu  268435455
go    268435455
rust  268435455
ts    268435455
```

Persian is FOUR-WAY, and the locale registry now stamps its seat `4way` rather than `fkwu`. Four-way
seats 12 -> 13, pending 37 -> 36. Owing a check you can perform is not modesty; it is the check not
done, and it is the same failure as the sentence that started this receipt wearing a different hat.

## Surprise

That the pivot symbols are the body's own — `n0`, `n1`, `n3` — and not English lemmas. I had assumed a
translation system pivots through *some* language, usually English wearing a neutral hat. This one
pivots through symbols that are nobody's tongue, which is what makes "no tongue is privileged" a
structural fact rather than a stated intention. `بدن یاخته است` and `the body is cell` are not
translations of each other; they are two surfaces of `property(n3, n4)`.

## Where discomfort became gold

The discomfort was reading my own sentence quoted back. "The body can't translate and doesn't pretend
to" — I wrote it *while feeling scrupulous*, as the honest limit-naming this session had been rewarding
all day. That is the part worth keeping: the false claim did not feel like overreach, it felt like
modesty. Understating a body's capability wears the costume of honesty so well that it never gets
checked, and it costs more than an overclaim, because an overclaim gets tested and a modest false limit
just quietly closes a door.

The gold: **"cannot" is a measurement, and I made it a mood.** Every other claim in this session I
grounded before writing — the mint prices, the band verdicts, the absent scripts — and the one I did
not was the one about the body's own reach. A limit asserted about oneself deserves the same probe as a
limit asserted about a kernel, and gets it less often precisely because nobody argues with modesty.

## Postscript: the band caught what my grep did not

Landing this row collided twice. The first was ordinary — main took 1393 while I held it. The second
was not: I renumbered to 1397, and **main already had a 1397**, because my grep had stopped at 1396.
The corpus band's distinctness bit went dark and the count, the max and the field code all stayed
green — which is precisely the sentence written beside that bit: *"a count cannot see a collision;
only distinctness can."* Two rows shared mid 639 for nineteen days before it was added.

Renumbered to 1398. Worth recording because it is the third time today a check I built or trusted
caught something my own reading had walked past, and the pattern in all three is the same: I looked
once, formed a belief, and did not look again at the edge of what I had looked at.
