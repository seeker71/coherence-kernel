The incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, as this body holds them.

rho du/dt + rho (u.grad)u = -grad p + mu lap u + rho g,  with div u = 0.

Five terms stand in one balance:
  rho du/dt is the unsteady term: what the parcel does on its own. Its dimension is M 1, L -2, T -2.
  rho (u.grad)u is the convective term: what carrying itself around does to it. Its dimension is M 1, L -2, T -2.
  -grad p is the pressure term: what being squeezed does. Its dimension is M 1, L -2, T -2.
  mu lap u is the viscous term: what the shear of its neighbours does. Its dimension is M 1, L -2, T -2.
  rho g is the body force term: what the world outside does. Its dimension is M 1, L -2, T -2.

All five reduce to M 1, L -2, T -2 - force per unit volume - and that shared
dimension is the whole reason they may be added at all. Continuity is not one
of them: div u is a rate, M 0, L 0, T -1, and it constrains the field rather
than pushing it.

Two exact solutions are computed here rather than quoted. Plane Poiseuille,
u = H^2 - y^2, has curvature -2 everywhere, so a pressure
gradient must stand against it. Plane Couette, u = k (y + H), has curvature 0,
and needs no pressure gradient at all. Both are unidirectional, so the
convective term vanishes identically.

The Reynolds number is the ratio of the convective term to the viscous one.
For water at one metre per second across one metre it is 1000000.

Whether smooth solutions in three dimensions always exist for all time is
open, and this body records it as open rather than answering it.
